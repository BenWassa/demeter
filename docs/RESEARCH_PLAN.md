# Project Demeter — Research Plan

## Purpose

Demeter will eventually make recommendations about places, land, infrastructure, access, and cost. Those recommendations are only useful if durable knowledge is separated from volatile facts and jurisdiction-specific rules.

This document defines what must be researched, how evidence should be stored, and what should never be presented without a freshness date.

## Evidence Classes

### A. Durable system knowledge

Examples:

- how a drilled well and pressure tank work;
- what a septic tank and drain field do;
- what an inverter and battery do;
- why backup heat matters;
- what orchard / greenhouse / pasture systems require spatially.

Preferred sources:

- provincial/federal technical guidance;
- extension services;
- utility / standards bodies;
- reputable engineering / agricultural institutions.

Review cadence: low frequency unless standards change.

### B. Jurisdictional rules

Examples:

- zoning and permitted uses;
- agricultural-land restrictions;
- septic approvals;
- well rules;
- building permits;
- accessory buildings;
- livestock / animal bylaws;
- conservation / floodplain restrictions.

Preferred sources:

1. municipality / regional district;
2. provincial regulator or ministry;
3. applicable conservation authority / health authority;
4. secondary interpretation only as supporting context.

Review cadence: at least annually for active candidate regions and whenever a property decision depends on the rule.

### C. Volatile market and logistics data

Examples:

- property prices;
- farmland values;
- mortgage assumptions;
- contractor cost ranges;
- solar/battery pricing;
- train / flight schedules;
- internet availability;
- incentives / rebates.

Preferred sources:

- official operators;
- current market data providers;
- current listing samples;
- dated contractor / installer data when primary public data is unavailable.

Every display must carry a retrieval date.

## Region Research Template

Every region profile should eventually contain the same minimum evidence structure.

### 1. Geography

- candidate subregions / towns;
- typical drive time to grocery;
- typical drive time to hospital / emergency department;
- access to a major employment city;
- airport / rail options where meaningful;
- road / winter-access considerations.

### 2. Climate

- January and July normal temperatures;
- extreme cold / heat context;
- frost-free or growing-season proxy;
- precipitation pattern;
- snow burden;
- drought / wildfire conditions where relevant.

Use Environment and Climate Change Canada or provincial primary data where possible.

### 3. Land and Property

- representative acreage price bands;
- current fixer-upper examples;
- current improved-homestead examples;
- farmland-value context;
- parcel-size availability;
- common building stock;
- common servicing pattern: well, septic, hydro, propane, etc.

Avoid a single listing as “the market.” Use samples and label them as snapshots.

### 4. Productive Land

- soil / agricultural capability context;
- topography;
- drainage / flood considerations;
- typical woodlot / field mix;
- growing potential;
- agricultural restrictions or protections.

### 5. Regulation

- residential use on rural / agricultural land;
- secondary dwellings;
- accessory buildings / barns / workshops;
- livestock rules;
- on-site wastewater rules;
- wells;
- conservation / development permits;
- subdivision restrictions;
- off-grid energy rules where relevant.

### 6. Hazards

Region-specific only; do not force every hazard into every region.

Potential categories:

- flood;
- wildfire;
- drought;
- erosion / slope;
- coastal / seismic;
- winter road access;
- water scarcity / poor well yield.

### 7. Work and Connectivity

- broadband / fibre availability patterns;
- cellular coverage caveats;
- major employment centre access;
- coworking / business services if relevant;
- realistic once-weekly commute possibilities.

### 8. Homestead Fit

Research should culminate in an explicit trade-off statement, not a generic score.

Questions:

- What does this region make unusually easy?
- What does it make unusually expensive or difficult?
- Which preference profiles should rise here?
- Which preference profiles should avoid it?

## Initial Region Backlog

### Southwest Ontario

Candidate corridor:

- Norfolk County
- Oxford / Tillsonburg / Norwich
- Elgin / St. Thomas / Aylmer
- Middlesex / London / Strathroy / Glencoe

Research priorities:

- acreage price gradient with distance from London / Hamilton / GTA;
- hospital / grocery access by candidate town;
- agricultural zoning and livestock flexibility;
- conservation-authority constraints;
- well / septic prevalence and replacement economics;
- London ↔ Toronto work-travel practicality;
- winter and growing-season profile;
- high-quality internet availability on rural concessions.

### Fraser Valley, BC

Candidate corridor:

- Mission
- rural Abbotsford
- Chilliwack
- Agassiz / Kent

Research priorities:

- Agricultural Land Reserve implications by property type;
- acreage cost premium;
- floodplain / river considerations;
- rainfall and drainage;
- wildfire exposure at valley edges;
- West Coast Express / Vancouver access where relevant;
- hospital / town access;
- smallholding and residential-use flexibility.

### Central / South Vancouver Island

Candidate corridor:

- Cowichan Valley
- Cedar / south Nanaimo
- selected inland or rural communities with strong service access

Research priorities:

- acreage availability and price bands;
- water / well and summer drought considerations;
- wildfire / interface risk;
- agricultural land and zoning;
- Nanaimo / Victoria access;
- Vancouver travel burden;
- growing-season advantage;
- local hospital / service access.

## Systems Research Backlog

### Water

Research:

- well types;
- well records and due diligence;
- yield and storage concepts;
- pumps and pressure tanks;
- treatment / UV / filtration;
- outage resilience;
- rainwater use for irrigation;
- freeze protection.

### Wastewater

Research:

- conventional septic system anatomy;
- inspection / pumping / failure signs;
- replacement fields;
- advanced treatment systems;
- siting relationships with wells, water bodies, buildings, and traffic;
- provincial / local approval pathways.

### Electricity

Research:

- grid-connected vs grid-optional vs off-grid architectures;
- solar resource by region;
- battery sizing concepts;
- generator backup;
- critical-load panels;
- outage strategy;
- current installed cost ranges;
- code / professional boundaries.

### Heating

Research:

- building-envelope priority;
- cold-climate heat pumps;
- wood heat;
- propane / fuel backup;
- generator interaction;
- freeze / outage failure scenarios.

### Food

Research:

- intensive garden area requirements;
- greenhouse sizes;
- orchard spacing;
- berries;
- compost;
- water demand;
- food storage;
- season extension;
- realistic household contribution rather than “self-sufficient” marketing claims.

### Livestock

Start with tiers rather than species overload.

- Tier 0 — none
- Tier 1 — chickens / bees
- Tier 2 — meat birds / ducks / small livestock
- Tier 3 — goats / sheep / pigs
- Tier 4 — cattle / horses / serious pasture operation

For each tier:

- space;
- shelter;
- fencing;
- feed storage;
- manure;
- water;
- winter workload;
- ability to travel;
- zoning / bylaw issues.

## Blueprint Research Backlog

The authored blueprint should gradually become evidence-based.

For each scenario (3, 10, 20 acres), define:

- parcel geometry assumption;
- road orientation;
- house / drive footprint;
- well / septic conceptual separation;
- septic reserve area;
- intensive-food area;
- orchard area;
- greenhouse area;
- shop / barn footprint;
- livestock tier supported;
- pasture allocation;
- woodlot / privacy buffer;
- solar exposure zone;
- irrigation / storage;
- snow / service access.

Never present an authored blueprint as a site-engineered plan.

## Cost Model Backlog

Demeter should eventually model cost in layers rather than one fake total.

### Acquisition

- property purchase;
- inspection / due diligence;
- financing / closing;
- immediate safety / habitability reserve.

### Critical systems

- well repairs / treatment;
- septic repair / replacement;
- roof / envelope;
- heating;
- electrical;
- generator.

### Resilience upgrades

- solar;
- batteries;
- water storage;
- wood heat;
- insulation / air sealing.

### Productive land

- garden;
- greenhouse;
- orchard;
- fencing;
- coop / barn;
- compact tractor / equipment where justified.

Every cost range should include:

- jurisdiction / region;
- year;
- source;
- whether labour is professional or DIY;
- whether tax / site prep / permitting is included.

## Source Record Format

Recommended shape:

```json
{
  "id": "source-id",
  "title": "Source title",
  "publisher": "Organization",
  "url": "https://...",
  "jurisdiction": "Ontario",
  "publishedAt": "2026-01-01",
  "retrievedAt": "2026-08-19",
  "tags": ["septic", "regulation"],
  "notes": "What this source actually supports"
}
```

## Research Quality Rules

- Prefer primary sources for technical and regulatory claims.
- Cross-check expensive decisions with more than one source.
- Do not promote a contractor blog into a province-wide rule.
- Do not convert one real-estate listing into a market average.
- Keep illustrative prototype values visually distinct from researched values.
- Record uncertainty where evidence is weak or highly parcel-specific.
- Store the “why” behind a recommendation, not only the score.
