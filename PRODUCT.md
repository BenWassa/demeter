# Project Demeter — Product Context

## Register

Hybrid web product.

- **Persuade / Experience:** landing and regional hero surfaces should make the life feel tangible and worth exploring.
- **Operate / Read:** planner, comparison, systems, blueprint, and roadmap surfaces should help a user make decisions and understand trade-offs quickly.

## Platform

Responsive web. Mobile is a first-class planning and browsing surface; desktop should reward deeper comparison and blueprint exploration.

The first prototype should remain lightweight and deployable on GitHub Pages. Framework adoption is a later implementation decision, after the interaction and visual model prove themselves.

## Users

Primary initial user: a Canadian professional exploring a comfortable, semi-self-sufficient rural life over a 5–10 year horizon.

The target user is not seeking wilderness survival or ideological self-sufficiency. They want a resilient, attractive homestead with normal comfort, reliable internet, healthcare and grocery access, and enough land to meaningfully grow food, build systems, and gain privacy.

Future audience: Canadians comparing rural regions, property types, and degrees of off-grid independence before making a major lifestyle or property decision.

## Product Purpose

Project Demeter turns an ambiguous aspiration — “live more independently on land in Canada” — into a visual, research-backed plan.

It should answer four questions:

1. **Where could this life work?**
2. **What property and land configuration fits the user?**
3. **What systems are required for water, wastewater, energy, heat, food, access, and work?**
4. **What phased path makes the transition financially and practically realistic?**

## Core Mechanism

Demeter translates lifestyle preferences into a connected recommendation across:

**region → property archetype → land use → infrastructure systems → cost/risk profile → phased roadmap**

The distinctive value is the connection between those layers. Region comparison without land planning is incomplete; an attractive blueprint without climate, zoning, water, access, and economics is fantasy.

## Initial Planning Profile

Use this as the seed scenario for the first working experience, not as a permanent assumption for every user.

- Comfortable semi-off-grid / grid-optional homestead.
- Self-sufficiency: moderate to high, roughly 5–7/10.
- Privacy: moderate to high, roughly 5–7/10.
- Comfort and modern amenities: high, roughly 7–9/10.
- Food production: meaningful but not full subsistence, roughly 5–7/10.
- Willingness for physical work: moderate to high.
- Land aspiration: potentially 10–25 acres, with usable land more important than nominal acreage.
- Starting property preference: an older or imperfect rural property with good bones and existing infrastructure, while retaining interest in build-from-scratch economics.
- Access: groceries and hospital ideally within ~30 minutes; major city access should remain practical for occasional work.
- Climate: avoid the harshest Canadian winters and extreme remoteness.
- Employment: remote-first professional work with minimal city attendance.

## Initial Regions

The first comparison set is intentionally small:

- Southwest Ontario
- Fraser Valley, British Columbia
- Central / South Vancouver Island

Additional candidates should enter only when they offer a materially different trade-off profile.

## Jobs To Be Done

### Explore

“When I imagine this life, show me what it could actually look and feel like in different parts of Canada.”

### Compare

“Help me understand which region or property type gives me the best balance of climate, cost, access, land, and self-sufficiency.”

### Learn

“Teach me how the major homestead systems work without assuming I am already a farmer, builder, electrician, or rural-property expert.”

### Design

“Show me what 3, 10, or 20 acres can realistically hold and how the systems relate spatially.”

### Plan

“Turn this into a staged 5–10 year path instead of one overwhelming purchase or construction project.”

## Product Principles

1. **Make the dream concrete.** Visuals should carry real planning meaning, not exist as decorative lifestyle imagery.
2. **Useful land beats acreage theatre.** Soil, slope, sun, water, access, zoning, buildings, and layout matter more than the number of acres.
3. **Resilience over purity.** Grid-optional and redundant systems are often better than disconnecting from useful infrastructure for ideological reasons.
4. **Comfort is a requirement.** This product should not romanticize deprivation.
5. **Reality belongs beside aspiration.** Every beautiful regional or homestead view should connect to costs, constraints, maintenance, or trade-offs.
6. **Progressive complexity.** Explain systems at a beginner level first, then allow depth.
7. **Source consequential claims.** Costs, regulations, climate, travel times, and property-market claims should carry dates and sources.
8. **Separate stable knowledge from volatile data.** System explainers can be durable; property prices, regulations, schedules, and incentives need timestamped research.

## Content Voice

Grounded, intelligent, concise, visually literate, and practical.

Avoid prepper rhetoric, generic sustainability slogans, cottagecore fantasy, and corporate-dashboard language.

## Non-Goals

Demeter is not initially:

- a real-estate listing marketplace;
- a substitute for zoning, legal, engineering, septic, well, electrical, or building-code advice;
- a farm-management ERP;
- a crop-yield optimizer;
- an autonomous property valuation tool;
- a survivalism or disaster-preparedness product;
- a social network.

## Success

A useful Demeter session should leave the user with a smaller decision space: fewer viable regions, a clearer property archetype, an understandable system stack, a realistic acreage/layout picture, and an explicit next step.

The strongest qualitative success signal is: **“I can now picture the life and understand what I would actually have to buy, build, learn, and maintain.”**
